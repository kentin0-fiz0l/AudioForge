# Saturation User Manual

**AudioForge Saturation v1.0.0**

Multi-mode analog-style saturation (tape, tube, transistor)

---

## Overview

**Saturation** emulates analog warmth with:

- **3 Saturation Types**: Tape, Tube, Transistor
- **Drive**: 0-100% (amount of saturation)
- **Tone**: Tilt EQ (darken or brighten)
- **Mix**: Parallel saturation blending

---

## Quick Start

### Tape Warmth

1. **Type**: Tape
2. **Drive**: 30-50%
3. **Tone**: -10% (slightly darker)
4. Result: Vintage tape warmth

### Tube Overdrive

1. **Type**: Tube
2. **Drive**: 60-80%
3. **Tone**: 0%
4. Result: Tube amp-style overdrive

---

## Parameters

### Saturation Type

**Tape**: Soft compression + tape-like saturation (warm, smooth)
**Tube**: Asymmetric tube distortion (rich, creamy)
**Transistor**: Aggressive transistor clipping (punchy, hard)

---

### Drive

**Range**: 0-100%
**Description**: Amount of saturation

- **0-30%**: Subtle warmth
- **40-60%**: Moderate saturation
- **70-100%**: Heavy distortion

---

### Tone

**Range**: -100% to +100%
**Description**: Tilt EQ (darken or brighten)

- **-100%**: Darker, warmer
- **0%**: Neutral
- **+100%**: Brighter, edgier

---

## Use Cases

### 1. Master Bus Glue (Tape)

**Settings**: Tape, Drive 20%, Tone -5%, Mix 100%
**Result**: Cohesive, analog-sounding mix

### 2. Guitar Amp Sim (Tube)

**Settings**: Tube, Drive 70%, Tone +10%, Mix 100%
**Result**: Overdriven guitar amp

### 3. Drum Punch (Transistor)

**Settings**: Transistor, Drive 50%, Tone 0%, Mix 80%
**Result**: Punchy, aggressive drums

---

## Tips

- **Tape**: Best for subtle warmth (mix bus, vocals)
- **Tube**: Best for guitars, bass, synths
- **Transistor**: Best for drums, aggressive elements
- Use **Tone** to balance brightness after saturation
